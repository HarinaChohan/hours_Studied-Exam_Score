## Predicting Student Exam Scores Using Study Hours

### Problem

Students always wonder if there is a point in spending time studying for an exam. Are the number of hours studied significant enough to get a good score?

Hence, the objective will be to build a model to predict the score of a student in their exam based on how many hours they spent studying and also to see its correlation (whether positive or negative) and make analysis and conclusions through them.

Hypothesis: The Hours spent studying is correlated to the Exam score a student gets.

Null hypothesis is that the exam score received has no correlation to the hours a student spent studying.

### Reflection and reporting

All the data and plots and the model show that the number of hours spent studying is highly positively correlated to the exam score, hence, students should spend the time studying if they want a good exam score.

The mean residual is a very small number, hence the model is reliable and valid for this sample. However, the sample is quite small so for the data to be generalized, it needs to be a larger sample size.

In conclusion, the score a student gets in an exam is governed by the hours they spent studying for that exam, and therefore, it is possible to predict the score based on how many hours they have studied.
